## How I will position the Wild Coast study

My [Lab 2](module-2-gis.md#lab-2-port-st-johns-canyons) ended on a planning question: two shelf-incising canyons lie just outside the Pondoland Marine Protected Area, off Port St Johns. Module 4 is where that question meets planning. This is how I want to frame it in the [Zenodo article](learning-path.md#after-the-course-a-zenodo-article) I write over the holidays.

**An open-data screening note.** The study shows what freely available global data on ODP can, and cannot, tell us about a real conservation question on the Wild Coast, and it ends on a proposal others can test. What is new is the open, reproducible route from a global file to a specific question at the edge of an MPA. It does not discover the canyons, and it does not by itself justify a new boundary.

**Why this framing.** The seafloor map I used is global, at about 1 km resolution. South Africa's own marine mapping is far more detailed, notably the National Biodiversity Assessment (2018) and the work behind the 2019 MPA expansion. It very likely knows these canyons, and the Pondoland boundary may have been drawn where it is for a reason, such as fishing access near Port St Johns. So the strong claim is a question: *open global data flag two near-shore canyons just outside Pondoland; was that boundary a deliberate choice, and does the evidence support extending it?*

| **3. Justice lens (discussion only)** | Who uses this coast, who decides its boundaries, the disputes over offshore exploration | My wider network | Needs community voices and sources; not the core without them |

The AI-assisted GIS gets one short paragraph in the methods, with an AI-use statement. The question, not the tool, is the story.

### Before I write

3. **Who to ask.** One email to someone at SANBI, the national environment department or a Wild Coast organisation before I publish a boundary proposal.
